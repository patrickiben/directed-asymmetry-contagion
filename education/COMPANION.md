# A complete reading companion to Anticipating Network Contagions

## How to use this document

This companion follows Patrick Iben's *Anticipating Network Contagions: When Directedness Outperforms Connectedness*, dated 5 July 2026, passage by passage. It is for a numerate reader who knows averages and graphs but is new to network control. The [concise study guide](STUDY_GUIDE.md) offers the shorter route. Here, work through the units in order, then use the glossary and answered transfer test.

The exact basis is the [author draft](../manuscript/anticipating_network_contagions_draft.pdf), SHA-256 b48f19b5a71943f540728fb197cb5d84aeea782a2aaad5a8b6144fe216755509. Page numbers refer to that sixteen-page PDF. [Complete coverage](COMPLETE_COVERAGE.md) maps its substantive passages, equations and figures to the units below. This is a teaching explanation of that frozen draft, not a new paper or an empirical rerun. Where the draft refers to supplementary analyses not printed in it, this document explains their stated role without inventing their contents.

These boundaries apply throughout:

- The stress series are empirical; the support interventions and cascade reductions are simulated on calibrated, rescaled linear surrogates.
- A predictive transmitter is not an identified physical cause or a potential-outcomes treatment effect.
- Five prediction-first cases lack an independently verified pre-analysis public timestamp. Two of those predictions failed.
- Most individual advantage intervals include zero. Only equity and the held-out COVID-19 network individually exclude it in the reported block bootstrap.
- The raw-data forecasting result is non-significant. It does not validate intervention benefits.
- The geometric account is interpretive. Neither a universal control theorem nor an operational emergency, clinical or financial policy is established.

Evidence labels distinguish **DEFINITION**, **ANALYTIC**, **MODELED_LOCKED** (a specified simulation), **MEASURED_DATED** (analysis of dated observations), **INTERPRETIVE** and **SCOPE**. A unit can contain more than one class; its evidence field keeps them separate.

## Reading the question and the model

### D01. What the abstract promises, and what it actually measures

**Where we are:** Abstract, page 1; overview diagram, Figure 1, page 14.

**What the passage is doing:** The abstract announces a comparison between two allocation rules. One supports the unit with the largest realized stress; the other supports the unit diagnosed as exporting stress-related predictive influence. Seven empirical networks provide the fitted dynamics. The controller experiments then occur inside those fitted models. The abstract also announces the three main verification objects: symmetrization, a prediction-first record and recovery of hypothesized historical origins. They are not three replications of a field intervention.

Worked reasoning: Reason it through: write a chain with three boxes: observations, fitted surrogate, simulated response. A reported cascade reduction belongs to the third box. A claim that Wisconsin has a fitted transmitting role belongs to the middle box. A measured daily pollution value belongs to the first. Moving a statement between boxes changes its evidence. The abstract's strong language about structural leverage should therefore be read with the model qualifier attached, not as a measured benefit of a real response plan.

**In ordinary language:** A map can help you ask where to place support. But a contest played on that map is still a model experiment. Here the map comes from real records. The test of support takes place in a simplified system built from them. Keep those stages separate whenever you repeat a headline.

**Evidence status:** Mixed: MEASURED_DATED inputs and fitted diagnostics; MODELED_LOCKED intervention results; SCOPE for transfer. The abstract's approximate recovery probabilities are developed later, not estimates of a policy's success probability.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Seven networks mean seven successful interventions. They include different empirical outcomes, null cases, failures of prior domain predictions and uncertain surrogate advantages.

**Try it:** Rewrite “anticipation reduced real network damage” faithfully. **Answer:** “Transmitter-targeting reduced modeled cascades in specified calibrated surrogates; the draft tests when that advantage appears.” That version preserves both the allocation comparison and its evidence ceiling.

### D02. Interference changes the allocation question

**Where we are:** Introduction opening, page 1; Methods causal-scope statement, pages 9–10.

**What the passage is doing:** The introduction uses interference to motivate a network problem. Interference means that one unit's outcome can depend on what happens to other units, including their treatment. The no-interference part of SUTVA rules that dependence out. This paper does not estimate a formal causal effect under a treatment-assignment design. It uses the interference literature to explain why deciding where to support a system may require information beyond each unit's own condition.

Worked reasoning: A useful reasoning example needs no invented data. Imagine two connected towns, one receiving a disturbance and one passing it onward. Relief for current suffering and source control answer different questions. A policy could need both. The paper compares stylized resource-allocation rules under a fixed model budget; it does not assign moral value to the towns. Its “loudest” variable is the modeled stress measure, not a survey of clinical need, vulnerability or total harm.

**In ordinary language:** Helping one place may change what happens elsewhere. That makes a list of local damage incomplete for deciding how to limit spread. You may need to know which places pass stress onward. Still, knowing that spillovers are possible does not tell you how large a real intervention effect will be.

**Evidence status:** DEFINITION for interference and the operational decision; SCOPE for causal interpretation. The references to partial and cross-cluster interference are background, not a claim that this analysis implements those identification methods.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** A source ranking licenses withholding help from heavily affected people. Nor does fitting a directed model repair confounding or create randomized treatment evidence. The diagnostic “where might there be leverage?” remains distinct from “what effect would this intervention cause?”

**Try it:** A hospital has the greatest current burden. Does this paper show that resources should be diverted from it? **Answer:** No. A modeled source-control objective is not a complete welfare or triage objective. Such a decision would require intervention evidence, constraints and values that this study does not supply.

### D03. What is new relative to connectedness and network control

**Where we are:** Introduction literature positioning, page 2; References, pages 12–13.

**What the passage is doing:** The paper connects several existing tools: non-negative vector autoregression, forecast-error variance decomposition, connectedness tables and model predictive control. It does not claim to invent those ingredients. Its proposed contribution is the control-testing layer: use a case-specific symmetrization experiment and a declared prediction record to ask when a directed diagnosis beats reacting to the largest reading. It positions this directed question alongside centrality, immunization and structural-control research.

Worked reasoning: Reason it through by comparing outputs. A descriptive connectedness analysis returns rankings or a matrix. A control experiment returns outcomes under different support rules. A prediction-first record returns a comparison between expectations and later findings. These outputs answer different questions. Merely displaying arrows on a network is not the added control experiment. Conversely, a controller's success without testing why it succeeds would not establish that direction supplied the advantage.

**In ordinary language:** A new study can use familiar tools to ask a more specific question. Here the question is not simply which node looks important. It is whether the measured direction of influence helps a support rule win a fair model comparison. Credit the tools separately from the proposed test.

**Evidence status:** INTERPRETIVE positioning supported by cited methods; DEFINITION of the study's verification objects. The draft's broad novelty statements are the author's claims, not a comprehensive independent priority review by this companion.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Every directed estimator is new here, or earlier centrality work ignored all useful structure. The paper explicitly compares several established source-aware alternatives and sometimes finds them as good as or better than its named score.

**Try it:** Which comparison most directly tests the proposed mechanism: a prettier connectedness plot, or repeating the allocation contest while reducing asymmetry? **Answer:** The latter. It changes the hypothesized mechanism within the surrogate while retaining a stated comparison. The plot remains useful for describing what the test is changing.

### D04. Transmitter, receiver and loudest unit are different definitions

**Where we are:** Introduction definitions, page 2; Methods loudest-unit definition, page 9.

**What the passage is doing:** A transmitter has positive net directional connectedness: its fitted exports exceed its fitted imports. The diagnosed transmitter is the unit with the largest such value. A receiver has the opposite balance. The loudest unit has the greatest realized stress under the domain's declared summary, such as mean or cumulative stress. These definitions do not force the transmitter and loudest unit to be different. Their possible coincidence becomes an important boundary case.

Worked reasoning: Hypothetical arithmetic, not a paper result: a node with exports 6 and imports 2 has net 4 in the stated connectedness units. A node with exports 3 and imports 5 has net −2. Nothing in those two calculations tells you either node's current pollution, incidence or financial loss. You need the stress series for that. Also inspect how “loudest” was summarized: a single peak can select a different unit from a long-run average.

**In ordinary language:** “Sends most outward” and “has the biggest reading” are different questions. A place can receive much more than it sends. Another can pass a disturbance along while its own reading remains modest. Sometimes the same place leads both lists. Measure the lists before assuming which story applies.

**Evidence status:** DEFINITION; the arithmetic is a teaching illustration. Fitted transmitter identities later become MEASURED_DATED diagnostic results conditional on the model and data window.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** A negative net value means a node is harmless, or a positive net value establishes a physical source. Net is a balance of fitted predictive contributions, not a complete causal inventory. A receiver can still transmit some influence and have major needs.

**Try it:** Could the transmitter be locally quiet and still be a poor real intervention target? **Answer:** Yes. Predictive importance need not be manipulable, and a feasible intervention may not act on the modeled quantity. That additional bridge is not established by the ranking.

### D05. The regularity has several distinct null cases

**Where we are:** Central claim, page 2; mechanism, page 5; falsifiability, page 6.

**What the passage is doing:** The draft associates an anticipatory advantage with directed asymmetry and a transmitter–loudest gap. It names three ways the advantage can disappear: near symmetry, weak coupling, or a transmitter that is already the loudest unit. These are not interchangeable diagnoses. Near symmetry means little net directional distinction; weak coupling means limited cross-unit influence; coincidence means the two compared rules can choose the same target despite strong direction.

Worked reasoning: Reasoning with rule identity is especially important. If both rules select the same node under the same support schedule, that comparison contains little or no targeting contrast. It does not refute the existence of direction. Influenza illustrates this distinction later. Flight delays illustrate a different limitation: substantial overall connectedness with near symmetry. Armed conflict illustrates weak measured coupling and a problematic pooled domain. A single scalar called “network importance” would hide these differences.

**In ordinary language:** Two rules cannot gain much from choosing different places when they choose the same place. Nor can a source rule help much when there is little spread to intercept. A system with no stable upstream direction is a third case. Similar small benefits can therefore have different explanations.

**Evidence status:** INTERPRETIVE regularity evaluated by MODELED_LOCKED experiments and empirical diagnostics. It is not presented as a theorem proved for all non-negative matrices, all budgets or all nonlinear systems.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Every asymmetric network must yield a useful real control benefit. The source defines a falsifiable empirical pattern over its tests, while model specification, uncertainty and intervention feasibility still matter. Avoid turning its strong verbal formulation into a universal guarantee.

**Try it:** A strongly directed network gives no difference because the source is also the loudest node. Is that the same result as a near-symmetric network? **Answer:** No. The observed controller tie may look similar, but one lacks a target contrast while the other lacks the diagnosed directional leverage.

### D06. Equation 1: what a first-order VAR says

**Where we are:** Pipeline, page 3; Methods equation 1, page 9; Figure 1, page 14.

**What the passage is doing:** Equation 1 is x_t = Φ x_(t−1) + ε_t. The vector x contains all units' stress readings at a time. The matrix Φ maps the previous time's readings into predictions of the next. The residual ε is what that linear prediction leaves unexplained. “First-order” means one modeled lag, not one unit or one cause. Each domain supplies its own stress series: incidence, depreciation, pollution, delay or fatalities.

Worked reasoning: To reason about one matrix entry, hold the other prior readings fixed within the fitted equation. A larger coefficient means more predictive response to that earlier variable in that model's scale. It is not automatically the effect of an intervention that changes the real variable. Units, transformations, omitted drivers and temporal resolution all influence the fitted relation. The model can be useful as a controlled testing substrate while remaining a coarse description of the original system.

Figure 1 should therefore be read left to right. Calibration is not diagnosis, and diagnosis is not observed intervention. The diagram's final box explicitly puts interdiction on a supercritical surrogate. Skipping that label would change the study's claim.

**In ordinary language:** The model uses the last set of readings to predict the next set. Each predicted reading can depend on several earlier readings. What the rule misses becomes its residual. This gives a common language for different data, but it does not make their underlying biology or physics the same.

**Evidence status:** DEFINITION of a standard statistical model; MEASURED_DATED fitting; MODELED_LOCKED downstream use. No mechanistic identity across domains is established.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** A coefficient matrix is a verified pipe, contact or transaction network. This analysis learns predictive dependencies from aggregate series.

**Try it:** Does “first-order” mean the model captures only direct physical neighbors? **Answer:** No. Any included prior unit may help predict another. The lag restriction concerns time, not verified spatial or physical adjacency.

### D07. Non-negativity, ridge and the choice of series

**Where we are:** Pipeline, page 3; Methods calibration, page 9; Limitations, pages 8–9.

**What the passage is doing:** The fitted coefficients satisfy Φ_ij ≥ 0, and ridge regularization stabilizes estimation by penalizing large coefficients. The analysis assesses stationarity per series and uses levels or differences accordingly. These are substantive modeling choices, not merely software settings. A non-negative linear model cannot represent an inhibitory lag in the same way as an unconstrained model. Differencing changes the target from a level to a change.

Worked reasoning: Consider the short flight-delay series mentioned in the limitations: roughly 90 time points for 18 units. Many possible cross-unit coefficients must be estimated from limited temporal information. Shrinkage reduces instability, but it does not manufacture new observations. A ranking can remain fragile even after a fitting routine converges. Dependence over time also means that 90 rows are not 90 independent experimental replications.

The currency example supplies another warning. Its use of non-stationary levels is explicitly retained as a limitation. A companion should not silently repair that choice or describe all cases as equally well supported. The common pipeline provides comparability of procedure, not identical statistical difficulty.

**In ordinary language:** Restrictions can keep a model from chasing noise. They also limit what it can describe. A rule that permits only positive fitted links is built to study reinforcing spread. It may miss a real balancing response. Always ask which data scale and constraints produced a ranking.

**Evidence status:** DEFINITION and SCOPE of estimation; empirical diagnostics are conditional on these choices. The draft refers detailed stationarity results to its supplementary material rather than printing them all here.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Constraints remove all overfitting, prove the arrows causal or guarantee stable rankings. A short series and a persistent trend can still influence the result.

**Try it:** Would a transmitter ranking on levels necessarily equal one on changes? **Answer:** No. They ask about different variation. Record the transformation and test sensitivity instead of treating the two targets as interchangeable.

### D08. From residuals to a connectedness table

**Where we are:** Pipeline, page 3; Methods connectedness, page 9; Figures 1–2, page 14.

**What the passage is doing:** Generalized forecast error variance decomposition, or GFEVD, divides model forecast-error variance into contributions associated with different units' innovations. The fitted coefficient dynamics and residual covariance both enter this construction. The Diebold–Yılmaz table summarizes those directed contributions as “to,” “from” and net connectedness. Total connectedness aggregates sharing, while net connectedness compares outward and inward contributions for a particular unit.

Worked reasoning: A useful reading exercise is to cover the labels on a connectedness chart and identify its axis. A positive net bar is not a pollution concentration, a number of infections or a percentage reduction under support. Figure 2's middle panel uses net connectedness percentages; its left panel uses measured PM2.5; its right panel uses surrogate reductions. All three belong in the explanation, but they cannot be added or compared as if they had the same denominator.

Residual covariance matters because simultaneous unexplained movement can affect the decomposition. “Generalized” does not mean immune to common drivers, omitted variables or model error. The paper itself keeps predictive directional influence separate from intervention effects and asks for further causal-invariance checks in volatility settings.

**In ordinary language:** The fitted model asks which units' surprises help account for its forecast uncertainty. Adding those contributions gives an overall sharing measure. Comparing outward and inward contributions gives a role for each unit. The same overall total can hide very different balances among individual units.

**Evidence status:** DEFINITION of the diagnostic; MEASURED_DATED estimates from fitted series. The control implications require the subsequent MODELED_LOCKED tests.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** “Connectedness” is not a direct count of physical links. In particular, the largest total sharing does not identify the best support target.

**Try it:** Can two networks have similar total connectedness but opposite net-role patterns? **Answer:** Yes. An aggregate can stay similar while the directional distribution changes. The smoke/flight comparison and symmetrization test investigate precisely that distinction.

### D09. Equation 2: horizon integration and its evidence boundary

**Where we are:** Methods equation 2 and horizon statement, page 9; Limitations horizon sensitivity, page 8.

**What the passage is doing:** Forecast-variance contributions depend on how far ahead the model looks. Equation 2 writes an integral of net_i(H) over forecast horizon H. The draft states that maximizing this integrated net score gives the same transmitter identity as its fixed-horizon analysis in the networks it recomputed. It also reports stability over multi-step horizons H ≥ 4. The PDF does not provide a general proof that every possible network preserves its rank under every horizon range.

Worked reasoning: Reason it through without inventing measurements. One unit could have a strong immediate effect that fades; another could act through a longer chain. Their accumulated contributions need not rank the same at all horizons. Integration also needs a stated range and weighting to define a numerical score. When applying the idea elsewhere, those details belong in the analysis plan rather than being hidden by the integral symbol.

The source's phrase about eliminating a free horizon parameter should therefore be taught alongside its actual reported verification. The printed evidence is identity agreement on studied networks and referenced supplementary sensitivity analysis. No new horizon calculations were performed for this guide.

**In ordinary language:** A short look ahead and a long look ahead can tell different stories. The paper checks whether its leading source changes when it changes that view. In its reported cases, the selected source stays the same. Treat that as a useful check of those cases, not a promise about every new system.

**Evidence status:** DEFINITION of an integrated summary; MEASURED_DATED robustness claim for evaluated cases; SCOPE for generalization. The notation alone is not a universal rank-invariance theorem.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** A horizon choice can never matter, or that an unspecified integral automatically removes every tuning decision.

**Try it:** A new system changes its leading transmitter between short and long horizons. Should you suppress that result because equation 2 appears in the paper? **Answer:** No. Report the instability, specify the horizon target and investigate whether a single static source summary is appropriate.

## Making the allocation experiment interpretable

### D10. “Loudest” depends on the declared observation summary

**Where we are:** Introduction, page 2; Methods loudest definition, page 9; smoke traces, Figure 2a, page 14.

**What the passage is doing:** The paper operationalizes loudest as the greatest realized stress, summarized by cumulative or mean series depending on the domain. It specifically warns that a peak can select a different state from a mean in the smoke data. This choice matters because the reactive comparator is defined through that target. Changing the summary changes the rule being compared, even if the fitted network is left untouched.

Worked reasoning: Think through the Figure 2 traces. Tall isolated spikes and sustained elevations convey different features of a pollution episode. A maximum emphasizes the worst instant. An average emphasizes the typical level over the chosen window. A cumulative summary depends on the time span and observation spacing. None automatically measures population harm, because exposure, population and vulnerability are not supplied by the stress ranking alone.

This also clarifies “reactive.” The paper's comparator is a particular damage-weighted heuristic with a fixed support budget. It is not a trial of every emergency-response procedure that responds to damage. A strong comparison names the implemented rule rather than treating its label as an entire field of practice.

**In ordinary language:** The place with the sharpest bad day need not have the largest burden over the whole period. Decide which meaning you intend before ranking places. Then use that meaning consistently. A ranking is not wrong merely because it answers a different question, but it must be named honestly.

**Evidence status:** DEFINITION and SCOPE. The domain-specific selections are reported analysis choices; their ethical or operational superiority is not evaluated.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** The labeled loudest node has the greatest population exposure or deserves the most assistance under all objectives. Those are different measurements and decisions.

**Try it:** If you replace the mean with a peak and get another “loudest” state, have you disproved the transmitter calculation? **Answer:** No. You changed the reactive target definition. Report both definitions and compare the resulting allocation tests under the same conditions.

### D11. Why rescale the fitted matrix to a common growth regime?

**Where we are:** Pipeline, page 3; Limitations, pages 8–9; Methods interdiction, page 10.

**What the passage is doing:** Native fitted models differ in stability. Some are stable; the smoke fit is reported near spectral radius 1.28. The controller experiments rescale each matrix to a mildly supercritical radius of 1.05 or 1.06. The spectral radius is the largest absolute eigenvalue. In this linear setting it helps describe whether repeated propagation tends to decay or amplify. The rescaling makes the cascade contests more comparable, while changing their distance from the native empirical dynamics.

Worked reasoning: A worked interpretation uses the same matrix in two roles. The fitted coefficients describe the calibrated surrogate before rescaling. A scalar multiplication then adjusts its growth tendency for the control contest. This preserves some structural relationships while changing time evolution and absolute cascade magnitudes. It is therefore reasonable to ask about a structural pattern across the contests, but unreasonable to report a percentage reduction as if the original system had undergone that support intervention.

The draft says the qualitative pattern survives its radius sensitivity checks. Those supplementary checks are not re-created here, and their claim does not make the absolute results invariant. Ratios such as “2.5 times” belong to a specified model regime too.

**In ordinary language:** A controller has little to stop if the model settles down by itself. The paper sets up a common, mildly growing challenge so the rules face a comparable test. That is a deliberate experiment on a model. It is not a finding that every real network was growing at that rate.

**Evidence status:** DEFINITION of spectral radius; MODELED_LOCKED rescaling experiment; SCOPE for effect sizes.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** A near-unit fitted radius certifies a physical phase transition or measures a real disaster threshold. Persistence, trends and estimation can also produce a large fitted radius.

**Try it:** Can you use the 42% equity reduction as an estimated bailout effect after learning about rescaling? **Answer:** No. It is an outcome of the rescaled surrogate, not an identified effect of a historical or proposed bailout.

### D12. A fair controller contest needs a shared budget and baseline

**Where we are:** Pipeline, page 3; Benchmark, page 4; Methods interdiction, page 10.

**What the passage is doing:** The paper compares no action, the loudest-node heuristic, undirected correlation centrality, VAR out-strength, impulse-response spillover, static transmitter targeting and dynamic cross-entropy model predictive control. Each intervention allocates a fixed per-step budget in the surrogate. Cascade reduction is defined relative to the no-action model outcome, not to a clinical or financial utility function. A negative reduction means the simulated outcome is worse than no action.

Worked reasoning: A hypothetical interpretation, not a new simulation: if a controller increases the modeled cascade, its reduction is negative even if it gives useful local relief to one node. That tension reflects the chosen aggregate objective. It does not prove local assistance is inherently harmful. Objectives can disagree, and the study evaluates only its specified cascade objective.

MPC repeatedly searches possible support plans using its model. “Cross-entropy” names the stochastic search approach; it does not make the forecasts true or the plan globally optimal in every run. Static targeting is simpler because it computes a role and keeps using it. Comparing these rules asks whether added planning machinery improves the particular surrogate outcome beyond a good fixed diagnosis.

**In ordinary language:** To compare rules, give them the same resources and the same modeled problem. Then state what counts as improvement. A rule that receives more support or sees future information is answering a different contest. The no-action result is the reference point for the reported reductions.

**Evidence status:** DEFINITION of comparison rules and MODELED_LOCKED outcomes. Detailed implementation belongs to the deposited analysis, not an invented optimization recipe in this guide.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** No-action is an acceptable real emergency policy, or the simulation objective captures all harms and benefits of support. It is an experimental comparator.

**Try it:** Why keep budget and random seeds shared across controllers? **Answer:** They reduce avoidable differences in resources and simulated conditions, making the remaining outcome contrast more informative about the rule. They do not resolve model misspecification.

### D13. Equation 3 removes the antisymmetric matrix component

**Where we are:** “Isolating the asymmetry,” page 3; Methods equation 3, page 10.

**What the passage is doing:** Equation 3 is Φ(α) = αΦ + (1−α)(Φ+Φᵀ)/2, evaluated at α = 1, 0.75, 0.5, 0.25 and 0. Let S = (Φ+Φᵀ)/2 and A = (Φ−Φᵀ)/2. Then Φ = S+A, and substitution gives Φ(α) = S+αA. Before the later rescaling, S is unchanged while the antisymmetric part is multiplied by α. This algebra identifies exactly what the interpolation changes.

Worked reasoning: A symbolic worked example avoids false empirical precision. For two opposite matrix entries a and b, the symmetric contribution is their average. Their directional difference is scaled by α. At α = 1 the original difference remains; at α = 0 it vanishes. This statement is about matrix entries. It does not by itself prove that every forecast-variance statistic or controller outcome changes linearly with α.

The controller contest must be recomputed along the path. Simply drawing a symmetric diagram would not test the outcome mechanism. Nor should a smooth-looking curve be treated as independent evidence from each point: all points are transformations of the same fitted case.

**In ordinary language:** Pair each fitted direction with its reverse and average them. That makes a symmetric version of the matrix. The test moves from the original toward that version in stated steps. It asks whether the source-targeting edge fades as the directional imbalance is removed inside the model.

**Evidence status:** ANALYTIC for S+αA; MODELED_LOCKED for the controller response along the path. The latter depends on the simulation and rescaling.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** The operation experimentally removes real-world causal direction, or preserves every connectedness total. In particular, post-interpolation scalar rescaling can change the symmetric component's numerical magnitude.

**Try it:** What remains fixed in the algebra before rescaling? **Answer:** S, the symmetric matrix component. What is attenuated? A, the antisymmetric component. The separate radius adjustment must be included when describing the full simulation procedure.

### D14. What the symmetrization null controls, and what it does not

**Where we are:** Symmetrization narrative, page 3; equation 3 and radius adjustment, page 10; uncertainty, pages 8–9.

**What the passage is doing:** After each interpolation, the paper re-rescales the matrix to the chosen common spectral radius. Otherwise changing direction could also change the system's tendency to grow, making the controller comparison harder to interpret. The null is therefore a controlled model perturbation: compare outcomes as asymmetry changes at a fixed specified growth setting. The source reports that advantages collapse in confirmatory cases and remain absent in baseline cases.

Worked reasoning: Reason through the two operations in order. First, the unscaled blend retains S and reduces A. Second, a scalar multiplication fixes the radius. A precise explanation cannot claim that the final matrices have identical symmetric entries, identical residual effects, identical total connectedness and identical control landscapes. The controlled feature is the chosen radius; other derived quantities may change as the model is recomputed.

The draft also refers to phase-randomisation nulls in its discussion. Those provide another referenced robustness object, but the selected PDF does not spell out their full procedure or results. This guide records that role without supplying unprinted numbers. Reading a source completely includes recognizing when it delegates detail elsewhere.

**In ordinary language:** Changing one part of a model can accidentally change another. The paper resets the growth setting after reducing direction. That makes a loss of advantage harder to explain simply by an easier, shrinking cascade. It still remains a test within the fitted model.

**Evidence status:** MODELED_LOCKED mechanism check; ANALYTIC description of the operations; SCOPE regarding unprinted supplementary details.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** A successful symmetrization test proves all alternative explanations impossible or establishes a universal theorem. It isolates a specific aspect of a particular surrogate experiment.

**Try it:** Why is “same growth regime” more accurate than “everything except direction is identical”? **Answer:** The procedure explicitly fixes the radius, but interpolation and rescaling can affect other derived statistics. The narrower description states the actual control rather than promising unsupported invariance.

## Reading each empirical comparison

### D15. Figure 2 and the near-matched smoke–flight comparison

**Where we are:** Connectedness comparison, pages 3–4; Figure 2a–c and caption, page 14; Figure 4a, page 15.

**What the passage is doing:** Smoke has about 82% total connectedness; flight delays about 77%. This is a near match, not numerical equality. The smoke model has a Wisconsin transmitter and Michigan mean-loudest receiver. Its support contest gives roughly 10% reduction for transmitter targeting versus 4% for the loudest rule. Flight delays are near symmetric and controllers remain near baseline. The comparison illustrates why a high total sharing score alone does not predict an advantage.

Worked reasoning: Read all three Figure 2 panels separately. Panel a displays dated pollution traces in concentration units. Panel b displays fitted net connectedness. Panel c compares surrogate cascade reductions. Its other displayed methods also show that the named transmitter score is not the only competitive source-aware option. The caption attaches the important uncertainty qualifier: these are seed-averaged point estimates, and smoke's block-bootstrap advantage interval includes zero.

The approximate ratio 10/4 explains the stated 2.5-fold comparison. It is a ratio of modeled reductions in this regime, not a measured reduction in pollution or a portable multiplier for air-quality response. A small denominator can also make a ratio sound more dramatic than the absolute gap.

**In ordinary language:** Two systems can be strongly linked in total while organizing that link very differently. Smoke supplies a fitted upstream–downstream contrast. The flight example does not supply the same useful contrast. Look at the role pattern and model test, not just the total percentage.

**Evidence status:** MEASURED_DATED traces and fitted diagnostics; MODELED_LOCKED controller results. The source itself calls the pair structurally illustrative rather than individually decisive.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** The pair proves all high-connectedness networks behave differently only because of direction. It is one comparison, strengthened by a within-case perturbation and qualified by uncertainty.

**Try it:** Which phrase is faithful: “identical connectedness” or “near-matched total connectedness”? **Answer:** Near-matched. Retaining that distinction avoids overstating the experimental control supplied by the pair itself.

### D16. The equity anchor: large surrogate contrast, narrow interpretation

**Where we are:** “The clearest case,” page 4; Limitations, pages 8–9.

**What the passage is doing:** The equity case aggregates eight national indices weekly over 2007–2010. Total connectedness is reported as 81%; the United States has dominant net connectedness around +21%, while Hong Kong and Japan are receivers. The model support comparison is about +42% reduction for transmitter targeting and −7% for the reactive rule. The approximate advantage of +49 percentage points becomes −23 under the symmetrized experiment. Equity is separate from the six out-of-domain tests but included in the seven-network origin-recovery audit.

Worked reasoning: A numerical reading check distinguishes reduction from advantage. The 42% and −7% are each compared with no action. Their difference is roughly 49 percentage points. The subsequent +49 to −23 statement refers to that advantage under a model transformation. It should not be retold as an observed market return changing from a gain to a loss.

The historical origin matters to the recovery audit, but knowing a familiar crisis narrative can also shape expectations. The declared source hypothesis and null selection rule are therefore part of the evidence. The paper additionally names volatility-spillover concerns as a future causal-invariance check rather than treating the financial arrows as settled mechanisms.

**In ordinary language:** In this fitted financial simulation, the two rules have a large gap. The reactive rule even worsens the modeled aggregate outcome. The test becomes more informative when that edge reverses as the matrix is made symmetric. None of these numbers is a measured result of a bailout or a trading strategy.

**Evidence status:** MEASURED_DATED calibration and historical-origin comparison; MODELED_LOCKED effects. Equity and held-out COVID-19 are the two cases with individual advantage intervals excluding zero in the reported bootstrap.

Worked reasoning: Reason through two uncertainty sources. Simulation variation comes from the random cascade experiment. Estimation variation comes from fitting a network to a finite, dependent record. A controller can be consistently good on one fitted matrix yet have an uncertain advantage across plausible fitted matrices. That is why shared seeds and many simulated steps cannot replace resampling or other checks of the underlying data model.

The source states that only equity and held-out COVID-19 individually exclude zero in block-bootstrap advantage intervals. Teaching the other point estimates as established standalone effects would remove the principal limitation. It is possible to discuss their structural pattern while saying their individual magnitudes are imprecise.

**In ordinary language:** A stable-looking answer from one fit may still be uncertain. Limited data, common shocks and changing variability can affect which node leads the ranking. Check how much the answer moves when data or sensible choices change. A suggested future method is not evidence that the current result has already passed that test.

**Evidence status:** SCOPE and reported robustness analysis; OPEN future work on alternative estimators and causal invariance. The guide does not claim those future checks are completed.

**Do not accidentally conclude:** Ridge and non-negativity eliminate model uncertainty, or that a positive point estimate settles the sign of a real intervention effect.

**Try it:** A fitted network gives the same leader for several horizons but changes leader under bootstrap resampling. Are the checks inconsistent? **Answer:** No. One varies a forecast horizon on the fitted model; the other varies the sampled evidence used to fit it. They probe different sensitivities.

### D34. Persistence, scale and the outlook must stay bounded

Worked reasoning: A worked transfer plan starts with the new domain's observations and decision target. Check whether units, time bins and a reinforcing linear model make sense. ### D35. Availability statements are a reproducibility map, not a rerun certificate

**Where we are:** Availability of data and materials, page 11; Methods reproducibility, page 10.

**In ordinary language:** A good research record tells you which inputs and code produced each output. The maintained repository can change after the paper's snapshot.

**Evidence status:** SCOPE and reported reproducibility provisions. The source’s reproducibility provisions identify inputs and procedures; their description is not an independent execution of every computation.
